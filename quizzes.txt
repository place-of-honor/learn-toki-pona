TOKI PONA DRILLS
================

Plain-text copy of the drill corpus already used by this repository.
The JavaScript lesson data remains the executable copy.

1. Core sentence pattern
------------------------
focus: mi moku. soweli li lape.
Make complete statements with a subject and predicate; use the predicate marker except after the two special pronouns.

1.1 core-01
prompt: mi moku.
A. I eat.
B. You eat.
C. The food is good.
D. They sleep.
answer: A. I eat.
note: The speaker is the subject, and eating is the predicate.

1.2 core-02
prompt: Choose the complete sentence: You sleep.
A. sina lape.
B. sina li lape.
C. mi lape.
D. ona lape.
answer: A. sina lape.
note: The second-person pronoun takes its predicate directly, without the usual marker.

1.3 core-03
prompt: Choose: The animal is big.
A. soweli li suli.
B. soweli suli.
C. soweli e suli.
D. mi li suli.
answer: A. soweli li suli.
note: An ordinary noun subject needs the predicate marker before its description.

1.4 core-04
prompt: ona li pali.
A. They are working.
B. You are working.
C. It is a tool.
D. They want work.
answer: A. They are working.
note: The third-person pronoun can mean he, she, it, or they; context decides.

1.5 core-05
prompt: Choose: I know.
A. mi sona.
B. mi li sona.
C. sina sona.
D. jan sona.
answer: A. mi sona.
note: The first-person pronoun takes its predicate directly.

1.6 core-06
prompt: Which is a complete sentence meaning “The person is good” rather than merely a noun phrase?
A. jan li pona.
B. jan pona.
C. jan e pona.
D. mi li pona.
answer: A. jan li pona.
note: The marker separates the ordinary noun subject from the predicate.

1.7 core-07
prompt: Match each subject word to its usual English role.
mi = I, me, or we
sina = you
ona = he, she, it, or they
jan = person
note: These four subjects appear throughout the first lessons.

1.8 core-08
prompt: Match each common predicate to its broad meaning.
moku = eat or food
lape = sleep or rest
pali = do, make, or work
pona = good, simple, or improve
note: A Toki Pona word can fill several related grammatical roles; the sentence supplies the role.

2. Direct objects
-----------------
focus: mi moku e kili.
Put the direct-object marker between an action and the thing directly affected by it.

2.1 object-01
prompt: mi moku e kili.
A. I eat fruit.
B. The fruit eats me.
C. My food is fruit.
D. I am a fruit.
answer: A. I eat fruit.
note: The object marker identifies the fruit as what the speaker eats.

2.2 object-02
prompt: Choose: You look at the book.
A. sina lukin e lipu.
B. sina lukin li lipu.
C. sina e lukin lipu.
D. lipu li lukin e sina.
answer: A. sina lukin e lipu.
note: The book is the thing directly seen, so it follows the object marker.

2.3 object-03
prompt: ona li pana e telo.
A. They give or put water.
B. The water gives them.
C. They are in the water.
D. They want water.
answer: A. They give or put water.
note: The water is what is given, sent, or placed.

2.4 object-04
prompt: Which sentence says “The person builds a house”?
A. jan li pali e tomo.
B. jan pali li tomo.
C. jan li pali li tomo.
D. tomo li pali e jan.
answer: A. jan li pali e tomo.
note: The house is the thing produced or worked on.

2.5 object-05
prompt: mi kute e kalama.
A. I hear a sound.
B. I make a sound.
C. The sound hears me.
D. I am sound.
answer: A. I hear a sound.
note: The sound is the direct object of hearing.

2.6 object-06
prompt: Choose: The child wants food.
A. jan lili li wile e moku.
B. jan lili e wile moku.
C. jan li lili e moku.
D. moku li wile e jan lili.
answer: A. jan lili li wile e moku.
note: Wanting can take a direct object: the wanted thing follows the object marker.

2.7 object-07
prompt: Match each action to a typical object.
moku e kili = eat fruit
lukin e lipu = look at or read a document
kute e kalama = hear a sound
pali e tomo = make or work on a building
note: Each right-hand noun is directly affected by the action.

2.8 object-08
prompt: Match the short sentence to its practical meaning.
mi pana e telo = I give or put water
sina jo e ilo = you have a tool
ona li open e poki = they open a container
jan li sona e nasin = the person knows the way
note: The same object structure works with physical actions, possession, and knowledge.

3. Noun phrases
---------------
focus: jan pona. tomo mi. telo lete.
Put the head noun first, then let later words narrow or describe it.

3.1 noun-01
prompt: jan pona
A. a good person or friend
B. a person's goodness
C. a simple house
D. to improve a person
answer: A. a good person or friend
note: The first word names the kind of thing; the second describes it.

3.2 noun-02
prompt: Choose the noun phrase: my house.
A. tomo mi
B. mi tomo
C. tomo li mi
D. mi li tomo
answer: A. tomo mi
note: The building comes first, and the speaker narrows which building it is.

3.3 noun-03
prompt: telo lete
A. cold water or liquid
B. watery cold
C. hot water
D. to cool water
answer: A. cold water or liquid
note: The phrase names a liquid and then describes it as cold.

3.4 noun-04
prompt: Which phrase can mean “an important book or large document”?
A. lipu suli
B. suli lipu
C. lipu li suli
D. lipu e suli
answer: A. lipu suli
note: The document is the head; size or importance modifies it.

3.5 noun-05
prompt: ilo suno
A. a light-producing tool or lamp
B. the sun's machine
C. a broken tool
D. to illuminate a tool
answer: A. a light-producing tool or lamp
note: The head is a tool; the following word says what kind of tool.

3.6 noun-06
prompt: Choose: a small red fruit.
A. kili lili loje
B. loje lili kili
C. kili li lili e loje
D. lili kili loje
answer: A. kili lili loje
note: The fruit comes first, followed by both descriptions.

3.7 noun-07
prompt: Match each noun phrase to its likely meaning.
soweli lili = small animal
tomo pali = work building or workplace
tomo tawa = moving building or vehicle
ilo toki = communication device
note: Start with the broad head noun, then read each modifier as narrowing it.

3.8 noun-08
prompt: Match each phrase while keeping the head-first order in view.
mani mi = my money
moku pona = good or healthy food
jan pali = worker
nasin sin = new method or route
note: The final word does not replace the head; it tells you which kind of head is meant.

4. Negation
-----------
focus: mi sona ala. ilo li pali ala.
Negate the relevant word or predicate by placing the negator immediately after it.

4.1 negation-01
prompt: mi sona ala.
A. I do not know.
B. I know nothing exists.
C. Nobody knows me.
D. I know.
answer: A. I do not know.
note: The negator follows knowing and denies it.

4.2 negation-02
prompt: Choose: They are not working.
A. ona li pali ala.
B. ona ala li pali.
C. ona li ala pali.
D. ona pali li ala.
answer: A. ona li pali ala.
note: The negator goes directly after the denied predicate.

4.3 negation-03
prompt: mi ken ala lape.
A. I cannot rest.
B. I can avoid sleep.
C. I do not want rest.
D. Nobody lets me rest.
answer: A. I cannot rest.
note: The denied idea is ability, so the negator follows the ability word.

4.4 negation-04
prompt: Choose: I do not want this.
A. mi wile ala e ni.
B. mi ala wile e ni.
C. mi wile e ala ni.
D. ni li wile ala e mi.
answer: A. mi wile ala e ni.
note: The negator denies wanting; the object structure remains intact.

4.5 negation-05
prompt: jan li pona ala.
A. The person is not good.
B. There is no person.
C. The good person leaves.
D. The person fixes nothing.
answer: A. The person is not good.
note: The description of the person is what gets negated.

4.6 negation-06
prompt: Which sentence says the tool is not broken?
A. ilo li pakala ala.
B. ilo ala li pakala.
C. ilo li ala pakala.
D. pakala li ilo ala.
answer: A. ilo li pakala ala.
note: Place the negator after the broken predicate.

4.7 negation-07
prompt: Match each negative sentence to what it denies.
mi moku ala = I do not eat
sina lape ala = you do not sleep
ona li kama ala = they do not come
jan li toki ala = the person does not speak
note: The negator follows the action in each sentence.

4.8 negation-08
prompt: Match the scope of each negation.
mi ken ala pali = I cannot work
mi wile ala pali = I do not want to work
mi sona ala e nasin = I do not know the method
mi jo ala e mani = I do not have money
note: Its position tells you whether ability, desire, knowledge, or possession is denied.

5. Questions
------------
focus: sina moku ala moku? ni li seme?
Ask yes-or-no questions by repeating the uncertain predicate around negation; replace unknown information with the question word.

5.1 question-01
prompt: sina moku ala moku?
A. Are you eating?
B. What are you eating?
C. You are not eating.
D. Why are you eating?
answer: A. Are you eating?
note: The repeated positive-or-negative predicate asks for a yes-or-no answer.

5.2 question-02
prompt: Choose the yes-or-no question: Do they want to sleep?
A. ona li wile ala wile lape?
B. ona li wile ala lape.
C. ona seme li lape?
D. seme li wile e ona?
answer: A. ona li wile ala wile lape?
note: Repeat the uncertain desire predicate around negation.

5.3 question-03
prompt: ni li seme?
A. What is this?
B. Is this good?
C. Where is this?
D. This is nothing.
answer: A. What is this?
note: The unknown predicate is replaced by the question word.

5.4 question-04
prompt: Choose: What are you looking at?
A. sina lukin e seme?
B. seme li lukin e sina?
C. sina lukin ala lukin?
D. sina seme e lukin?
answer: A. sina lukin e seme?
note: The unknown direct object occupies the ordinary object position.

5.5 question-05
prompt: jan seme li kama?
A. Which person is coming?
B. Why is the person coming?
C. Is anyone coming?
D. What does the person bring?
answer: A. Which person is coming?
note: The question word modifies person, asking which person.

5.6 question-06
prompt: Choose: Where are you going?
A. sina tawa ma seme?
B. ma li tawa e sina seme?
C. sina tawa ala tawa?
D. seme li ma tawa?
answer: A. sina tawa ma seme?
note: An unknown place is expressed as which place in the normal destination position.

5.7 question-07
prompt: Match each question to the information it requests.
seme li lon poki? = what is in the container
sina wile e seme? = what you want
jan seme li toki? = which person is speaking
sina pali e seme? = what you are making or doing
note: The question word stays where the missing answer would normally appear.

5.8 question-08
prompt: Match each yes-or-no question to its plain-English reading.
sina pona ala pona? = Are you okay or good?
ilo li pali ala pali? = Does the device work?
ona li kama ala kama? = Are they coming?
telo li lete ala lete? = Is the water cold?
note: The repeated predicate presents its positive and negative possibilities.

6. Adult-life translation
-------------------------
focus: mi pali lon tomo pali. ilo mi li pakala.
Use the existing small vocabulary to say practical things about work, money, rest, tools, travel, and family.

6.1 adult-01
prompt: mi pali lon tomo pali.
A. I work at a workplace.
B. I build my house.
C. My workplace is broken.
D. I travel for work.
answer: A. I work at a workplace.
note: The context places the speaker's work inside a work building.

6.2 adult-02
prompt: Choose: My tool is broken.
A. ilo mi li pakala.
B. mi pakala e ilo.
C. ilo li pakala e mi.
D. ilo pakala li mi.
answer: A. ilo mi li pakala.
note: The possessive modifier identifies the tool, and the predicate describes its condition.

6.3 adult-03
prompt: tenpo ni la mi wile lape.
A. Right now I want to rest.
B. I rested before this.
C. I never have time to rest.
D. This rest will take time.
answer: A. Right now I want to rest.
note: The opening context sets the time as now.

6.4 adult-04
prompt: Choose: I do not have much money.
A. mi jo ala e mani mute.
B. mani mute li jo ala e mi.
C. mi mani ala e jo mute.
D. mi wile ala e mani.
answer: A. mi jo ala e mani mute.
note: Possession is negated, while much modifies money.

6.5 adult-05
prompt: mi tawa tomo kepeken tomo tawa.
A. I go home using a vehicle.
B. My vehicle becomes a house.
C. I build a vehicle at home.
D. The house moves toward me.
answer: A. I go home using a vehicle.
note: The means phrase says that a moving structure—a vehicle—is used for the trip.

6.6 adult-06
prompt: Choose: I want to repair or improve the device.
A. mi wile pona e ilo.
B. ilo li wile pona e mi.
C. mi wile e ilo pona.
D. mi pona li wile e ilo.
answer: A. mi wile pona e ilo.
note: Improving is the action applied to the device; wanting modifies that action.

6.7 adult-07
prompt: Match each practical sentence to its likely use.
mi wile moku = I need or want food
mi ken ala pali = I cannot work
o pana e ilo tawa mi = Please give or send me the tool
mi sona ala e nasin = I do not know the method or route
note: Small general words can carry the practical point without specialized vocabulary.

6.8 adult-08
prompt: Choose: I want to talk to my parent.
A. mi wile toki tawa mama mi.
B. mama mi li wile e toki mi.
C. mi wile e mama toki.
D. mi toki e mama mi.
answer: A. mi wile toki tawa mama mi.
note: The direction word marks the person toward whom the speaking is directed.

7. Rewrite the idea simply
--------------------------
focus: pali mute li lon. mi ken ala pali e ale.
Preserve the useful point of a complicated English thought instead of hunting for a one-word substitution.

7.1 simple-01
prompt: Your phone battery died. Which paraphrase keeps the useful point?
A. ilo toki mi li jo ala e wawa.
B. ilo toki mi li moli e suno.
C. mi wile ala toki.
D. wawa li toki e ilo mi.
answer: A. ilo toki mi li jo ala e wawa.
note: Describe the communication device as lacking power rather than inventing a special battery word.

7.2 simple-02
prompt: The machine malfunctions intermittently. Choose the clearest decomposition.
A. tenpo ante la ilo li pali. tenpo ante la ona li pali ala.
B. ilo li pakala tenpo.
C. tenpo li pali e ilo ante.
D. ilo ala li pali e tenpo.
answer: A. tenpo ante la ilo li pali. tenpo ante la ona li pali ala.
note: State the two observable conditions at different times.

7.3 simple-03
prompt: I am overwhelmed by too many tasks. Which version states the practical problem?
A. pali mute li lon. mi ken ala pali e ale.
B. mi li pali mute ale.
C. ale li ken ala e mi.
D. pali li pilin e mi mute.
answer: A. pali mute li lon. mi ken ala pali e ale.
note: Break the idea into much work existing and the speaker being unable to do all of it.

7.4 simple-04
prompt: We should reschedule. Choose the compact practical request.
A. o ante e tenpo.
B. tenpo li ante e mi.
C. o weka e tenpo ale.
D. mi ante ala ante?
answer: A. o ante e tenpo.
note: A command to change the time carries the action that matters.

7.5 simple-05
prompt: I need a break before continuing. Choose the two-step paraphrase.
A. mi wile lape. ni la mi ken awen pali.
B. mi awen lape li pini pali.
C. pali li lape e mi.
D. mi ken ala lape tan pali.
answer: A. mi wile lape. ni la mi ken awen pali.
note: First state the need for rest; then state that continuing work becomes possible.

7.6 simple-06
prompt: Please explain it more simply. Choose a concrete request.
A. o toki e ni kepeken nimi lili.
B. o lili e ni kepeken toki.
C. nimi li pona e toki mi.
D. o toki ala e nimi.
answer: A. o toki e ni kepeken nimi lili.
note: Asking for few or small words makes the desired simplification concrete.

7.7 simple-07
prompt: The plan depends on money we do not have. Which paraphrase exposes the dependency?
A. mi jo ala e mani. tan ni la mi ken ala kepeken nasin ni.
B. nasin ni li mani ala.
C. mani li jo e nasin mi.
D. mi wile ala e nasin tan mani.
answer: A. mi jo ala e mani. tan ni la mi ken ala kepeken nasin ni.
note: State the missing resource, then make the resulting inability explicit.

7.8 simple-08
prompt: Match each simple message to the more elaborate English idea it can carry.
mi sona ala. o toki sin. = I did not understand; please explain again
ilo li pali ala. = the equipment is not functioning
mi wile ante e nasin. = I want to change the approach
tenpo li lili. = we are short on time
note: The goal is a useful paraphrase, not a word-for-word cipher.
